# Which trade for which problem

## Plumber
Leaks, dripping taps, blocked drains or toilets, low water pressure, tank and pipe fittings. Tell them where the water is coming from and whether it stops when you close the tap or the main valve. Closing the tap or valve is a safe first step.

## Electrician
No power in part of the house, a fan or light that does not work, sparking, a burning smell, switchboards and wiring. Always a qualified person. Switch the device off at the socket and keep away. Do not open switchboards or touch wiring.

## Carpenter
Doors and windows that stick, hinges, locks, furniture repair, wardrobes, shelves. Tell them the size of the job and whether it is repair or new work; measured work is priced by the unit, small repairs often by the task.

## Painter
Walls, ceilings, damp patches, wood polish. Damp that keeps coming back may need a plumber first. Tell them the rooms and approximate area.

## Cleaner
Deep cleaning of a house, kitchen, bathroom or office, after-renovation cleaning. Tell them the size and whether any special equipment is needed.

## Domestic helper and caregiver
Regular household help, care for an elderly person or a patient. Say the hours, the days and any special care needs so the right worker is offered.

## Gardener
Garden upkeep, pruning, planting, lawn care.

## Technician
Appliances such as a washing machine, refrigerator or air conditioner. Gas and refrigerant work is for a qualified person only.

## Loading and unloading labour
Shifting, loading and unloading goods by a crew from a cooperative society. Tell them the load, the floors and whether there is a lift so the right crew size is offered.

## Prices
TARA only quotes what workers near you have actually published. If nobody has published a rate for a job, TARA says so and does not invent one.
